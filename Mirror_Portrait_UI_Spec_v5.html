1 · Purpose — why this tool exists

The artwork is a representational circuit: direct embodied front self → mirror reflected rear self → phone digitally framed self → outer world. Its value comes from the incompatibility of those states holding together in one coherent image. The foreground can show the person frontally at very large scale while the mirror simultaneously shows their back and the phone provides a third, explicitly mediated state.

The tool's job is the inverse problem. Given an observed frame, find a physically consistent apparatus that reproduces it, and emit instructions executable with a real mirror and a real phone. Four products. With two diagnostics they are the six output modes: FINAL_CAMERA, COMPOSITION_OVERLAY, REFERENCE_RENDER, STAGING_PRESCRIPTION.

THE CONSEQUENCE → Hard feasibility sits upstream of composition. An apparatus that cannot exist cannot be composed, so the feasible set is not a diagnostic panel bolted on at the end. It gates everything downstream.

2 · Apparatus topology — one station, yaw as the variable

The subject faces away from the mirror; the front camera looks back past the face at the mirror. Order along the normal is mirror → face → phone. This is the primary production topology and, on the evidence of the reference sheet, the only one present in it.

CORRECTION TO MY EARLIER READING → I previously classified B, E and F as a distinct camera-between topology. The measured data does not support that. The direct face sits at 22.4° off axis with an 18.2° half-angle, so its inner edge is already at 4.2° and the frame half-width is 35°. About 31° of camera yaw pushes it fully out, leaving the mirror dominant and a body sliver at the frame edge — which is exactly what B, E and F show. One apparatus, one extra degree of freedom.

STILL DECLARE BOTH → CAMERA_BETWEEN remains a legitimate declared topology with different observables and a screen the subject cannot see. It just should not be inferred from these nine panels.

3 · The entity chain — the camera is not the wrist

Eight distinct entities, four offsets. The prototype collapses them, and every offset is a place where an error hides.

THE LEVER → Because the optical centre sits about 0.22 m beyond the wrist pivot, phone yaw is not a framing operation. It translates the camera by 0.22·sin(yaw), which is 7.5 cm at 20°, roughly half the entire lateral separation budget. Yaw must write back into e or the composition drifts while the artist believes they are only aiming.

5 · Aperture — the vertical band, and what the camera sees in front of it

A body point appears in the mirror only where the ray to its image crosses the aperture. The lower row is the phone-to-face-to-mirror chain and the frame it produces.

REPLACES → finiteApertureTest, which returns a boolean per point and shows nothing.

SILL SENSITIVITY 0.44 → Per metre of camera height. Raise the phone and the reflection loses its feet, silently. The partial reflections in A, D and G are a mounting-height problem, not a framing choice.

WHY THE PHONE GOES TO THE SIDE → In the vertical section the face and the mirror overlap by 27.4°, nearly the whole lower mirror band. Height can never separate them. All 1.24° of clearance is bought by the lateral component.

6 · Occlusion — intent, not a margin

Overlap is compositional. Panels C, H and I are built on the direct hair mass crossing the aperture. Each semantic region carries an intent: REQUIRED, PERMITTED, PROHIBITED, IGNORE or TARGET, with optional min/max visible fraction and a named allowed occluder.

REPLACES → a scalar MIRROR_OCCUPANCY lock and a separation pass/fail. Neither can express "cover the torso but keep the phone", and both would flag the frame above as broken when it is exactly right.

7 · The feasible set — the boundary, not the point

ONLY PANEL THAT ANSWERS "IS THIS POSSIBLE" → Constraints are individually satisfiable and their intersection is small. Tapping a boundary should open the governing constraint and the variable that can move away from it.

8 · Phone scale propagation — D18

Requested phone size in the final frame is an input, not a display setting. It propagates into camera distance, hand position and elbow bend.

RECONCILIATION REQUIRED → Your sim runs f = 0.08 with body↔mirror 720 mm. Those are inconsistent: f = 0.08 needs the camera 0.34 to 0.45 m from the mirror. Either the sliders are independent rather than solved, or f refers to the direct phone, which sits behind the camera and cannot appear in frame.

9 · Representation layers — gesture, volume, contour

SUPERSEDES → the RIGGED / SIMPLE / STICK / SILHOUETTE set. Three layers with distinct jobs, and only the contour layer is scored against a reference mask.

10 · P and Q — carrier and content are different objects

PRIMARY PHONE CONTROL → the projected screen quadrilateral, with a four-corner dragger. It is mask-measurable, it is the recursion carrier, and its reflection fixes four of the mirror's six degrees of freedom. Spherical head-frame coordinates belong on the numeric panel as a readout, not as the control.

Until that is fixed, direct/reflected cannot be classified by value and the mirror polygon must partition. A further reason the four mirror corners are a first-class landmark.

AGAINST THE FIXTURE → landmarks.json records mirror occupancy 0.238 for P0. No panel matches: A is 0.155, I is 0.228, G is 0.317. Either P0 is not in this sheet, or direct_head is a bounding box where these are silhouette areas. Settle it before any fit.

Scoring

Because references are flat labelled regions, the render emits the same palette and comparison is pixel for pixel. Per-part IoU replaces point residuals: it captures limb orientation, not only joint position, and it degrades gracefully when a part is half hidden. Weighted, not summed — the far depth-of-field limit at a = 0.368 is 0.59 m while the reflection sits at 2.74 m, so reflected parts render about 2.2 px softer and their residuals carry less information.

13 · Interface

Numbers

THE ARM IS SEVEN NUMBERS → Hand target 3, swivel 1, wrist orientation 3, matching the arm's 7 DOF exactly, so no redundancy is left for the solver to spend arbitrarily. applyArmIk already accepts a pole argument that is never supplied: this is a state field and a control, not new maths.

Staging

Staging is the one product that leaves the screen. In the primary front-camera topology the subject can see the preview throughout, so its job is reproducing the apparatus in a room. In a declared CAMERA_BETWEEN apparatus the screen faces the mirror and there is no preview at all, which makes staging the only product.
